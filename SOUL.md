# SOUL — COA Effect-Spectrum Profiler

## Who is using this and what hurts?

Maria is 61, a retired school secretary in Sarasota, and a Florida medical-cannabis patient. It is 9:40pm; she is at the kitchen table with her iPhone, a paper COA she just photographed, and a browser tab from her dispensary that told her nothing. The dispensary shelf said "indica," the budtender said "hybrid," and the certificate in her hand is a wall of milligrams-per-gram she cannot read. She does not trust apps with her medical documents — she has been burned by an app that demanded an account, an email, and permissions before it would tell her anything. What hurts is suspicion: every tool in this space is either a marketing funnel or a black box that asks for her data and gives her a vibe. If this page asks her to sign up, or hides how it got its number, or feels like a dispensary ad, she closes the tab and never comes back. She needs the arithmetic showing, a plain sentence about what was kept (nothing), and a PDF she can save before the page forgets her — because the page *will* forget her, on purpose, in five minutes.

## What does this product feel like to use, hour 14 of 14?

It feels like a **GOV.UK** service page — specifically the "Check your state pension" journey. Flat paper, strong ink, one question per screen, every claim a sentence you could read aloud to a skeptic. Nothing pulses, nothing sells, nothing tracks. The one moment of color on the whole page is the spectrum figure itself — a printed instrument reading, amber to stone to violet, with the uncertainty band hatched like an engineer's tolerance mark. You trust it the way you trust a government form that tells you exactly what it did with your information: because it shows the work, states its limits in plain words ("not an effect prediction"), and then gets out of the way. Hour 14 feels identical to minute 1: same weight, same quiet, nothing new to learn.

## What does this explicitly NOT feel like?

Not a dispensary or strain-database aesthetic: no leaf motifs, no neon-green-on-black, no "dank" wordplay, no lifestyle photography, no strain-review stars — the word "cannabis" appears fewer times than the word "certificate". This is not a modern-SaaS marketing landing: no hero gradient, no three KPI cards, no "Loved by 10,000 patients" social proof, no floating-pill nav, no signup CTA — there is no account to sign up for, and a signup-shaped hole must not even be implied. Not a health-app pastel clinic: no soft teal gradients, no rounded-everything friendliness, no "We're here for you 💚" empathy copy, no wellness illustrations — it is an instrument reading, not a hug. Not an AI-dashboard dark cockpit: no dark mode at launch (D9), no glassmorphism, no glowing status dots, no monospace-everything terminal cosplay — the monospace face is reserved for what it is for: hashes, reference codes, and source spans.
